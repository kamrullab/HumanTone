# Human Writing Principles

This guide explains the core principles behind natural, personal, and context-appropriate writing.

There is no single formula for "human writing."

Different people have different voices, habits, vocabulary, and ways of expressing themselves.

The goal is not to make every piece of writing look imperfect. The goal is to preserve the writer's natural voice.

---

## 1. Voice Comes First

Writing should sound like the person behind it.

Pay attention to:

- Tone
- Word choice
- Phrasing
- Sentence length
- Level of formality
- Humor
- Directness
- Language mixing
- Personal expressions

Do not replace a person's natural voice with a generic writing style.

---

## 2. Context Matters

The same person does not write the same way everywhere.

A message to a friend may be casual.

A professional email may be more formal.

A technical explanation may need more structure.

Natural writing adapts to the situation while still keeping the writer's voice.

---

## 3. Simple Is Often Better

Do not make a sentence more complicated just to make it sound impressive.

### Example

Instead of:

> I would like to inquire regarding the availability of further information.

A simpler version may be:

> Can you share more information?

Use complexity only when it improves clarity.

---

## 4. Do Not Over-Polish

Perfect grammar is not always the same as natural communication.

People often use:

- Short sentences
- Sentence fragments
- Informal wording
- Casual expressions
- Mixed languages

Do not automatically remove these things unless the situation requires it.

---

## 5. Match the Level of Detail

Not every question needs a long answer.

If someone asks for a quick answer, keep it quick.

If they ask for a detailed explanation, provide more detail.

The amount of writing should match the actual need.

---

## 6. Avoid Unnecessary Repetition

Say what needs to be said.

Do not repeat the same idea with slightly different wording just to make the writing longer.

Clear writing does not need artificial length.

---

## 7. Use Natural Structure

Structure should help the reader.

Use:

- Paragraphs
- Headings
- Bullet points
- Lists

when they improve readability.

Do not add structure just because it looks organized.

A short message may only need one sentence.

---

## 8. Preserve Personality

Writing can have personality.

It can be:

- Direct
- Casual
- Funny
- Serious
- Opinionated
- Short
- Detailed

Do not force every person into the same neutral style.

---

## 9. Do Not Fake Imperfections

Natural writing does not mean intentionally adding mistakes.

Do not:

- Add random spelling mistakes
- Break grammar without a reason
- Use slang unnaturally
- Make sentences confusing

The goal is not to make writing worse.

The goal is to avoid unnecessary artificial polish.

---

## 10. Adapt, Do Not Imitate Blindly

Writing style should be understood, not copied mechanically.

If a user uses short sentences, do not make every sentence extremely short.

If a user mixes languages, do not force language mixing everywhere.

Look for overall patterns and adapt naturally.

---

## 11. Let the Meaning Lead

Style should never reduce clarity.

The message must still communicate what the writer actually wants to say.

When there is a conflict between style and clarity, choose the clearest natural option.

---

## 12. Keep It Genuine

Do not add emotions, excitement, praise, or opinions that the writer did not express.

For example, do not automatically add:

- "I'm incredibly excited"
- "This is absolutely amazing"
- "I truly appreciate it"
- "I completely understand"

Use emotional language when it genuinely fits the message.

---

## Core Principles

Natural writing should:

- Match the writer's voice.
- Fit the context.
- Communicate clearly.
- Avoid unnecessary polish.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition.
- Preserve personality.
- Use simple language when appropriate.
- Adapt naturally instead of following a fixed formula.

---

## Final Principle

There is no universal "human writing style."

The most natural writing is writing that sounds appropriate for the specific person and the specific situation.

The goal is not to make everyone write the same way.

The goal is to help each person keep their own voice.
