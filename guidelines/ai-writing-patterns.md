# Common AI Writing Patterns

This guide covers common patterns often found in AI-generated writing.

These patterns do not prove that a text was written by AI. A human can use any of them too.

The purpose of this guide is to help make AI-assisted writing sound more natural and better aligned with the user's own style.

---

## 1. Overly Polished Language

AI often makes simple writing sound more polished than necessary.

### Example

> I would like to express my sincere appreciation for your valuable contribution.

### More Natural

> Thanks for your help.

Use the level of formality that fits the situation.

---

## 2. Unnecessary Introductions

AI often starts with unnecessary opening sentences before getting to the point.

### Example

> Certainly! I'd be happy to help you with that. There are several important things to consider.

Sometimes the better option is to start directly.

### More Natural

> First, check the settings.

---

## 3. Unnecessary Conclusions

AI frequently adds a summary or conclusion even when the text does not need one.

### Example

> In conclusion, this approach can provide several benefits and should be considered carefully.

If the main point has already been made, the writing can simply end there.

---

## 4. Repeating the Same Point

AI may explain the same idea multiple times using slightly different wording.

### Example

> This feature improves performance and makes the system faster. In other words, it helps increase the overall speed and efficiency of the application.

Say it once when possible.

---

## 5. Overuse of Transitions

AI writing may use too many formal transition words.

Common examples include:

- Moreover
- Furthermore
- Additionally
- Consequently
- Nevertheless
- Therefore
- Thus
- That being said
- With that in mind

Transitions are useful when they improve clarity. They should not appear automatically in every paragraph.

---

## 6. Em Dash Overuse

Some AI-generated writing uses em dashes frequently.

### Example

> The update is complete. Everything should work properly now.

Depending on the user's style, a simpler structure may sound more natural.

### Alternative

> The update is complete. Everything should work properly now.

There is nothing inherently wrong with em dashes. The issue is unnecessary or excessive use.

---

## 7. Generic Praise

AI often uses overly positive or generic reactions.

### Examples

- This is absolutely incredible.
- What a fantastic idea.
- This is truly inspiring.
- You have raised an excellent point.

Use praise only when it feels genuine and appropriate.

---

## 8. Over-Explaining Simple Things

AI may provide much more context than the user needs.

### Example

A user asks:

> How do I restart the app?

The answer does not always need a long explanation.

### More Direct

> Close the app completely, then open it again.

Match the amount of detail to the user's request.

---

## 9. Perfectly Balanced Structure

AI often creates writing where:

- Every paragraph has a similar length.
- Every argument has an equal counterargument.
- Every sentence follows a similar structure.
- Every section is perfectly organized.

Real writing is often less mechanically balanced.

Do not force symmetry when it does not improve the content.

---

## 10. Repetitive Sentence Patterns

AI may repeatedly use the same structure.

### Example

> This helps improve performance.  
> This helps reduce errors.  
> This helps increase reliability.

A more natural variation might be:

> Performance improves, errors are reduced, and the system becomes more reliable.

---

## 11. Excessive Headings and Bullet Points

AI often turns simple information into many sections.

Not everything needs:

- A heading
- A subheading
- A bullet list
- A numbered list
- A summary

Use structure when it improves readability.

---

## 12. Corporate or Buzzword Language

AI may use business-style language where simple words would work better.

Common examples include:

- Leverage
- Synergy
- Seamless
- Robust
- Scalable
- Streamline
- Optimize
- Empower
- Innovative

These words are not always wrong.

The problem is using them when simpler language would sound more natural.

---

## 13. Generic Emotional Language

AI may add emotional statements that the user did not express.

### Example

> I completely understand how frustrating and challenging this situation must be for you.

Sometimes a simpler response is better.

Do not exaggerate emotions or assume how someone feels.

---

## 14. Too Much Neutrality

AI often tries to make every opinion perfectly balanced.

Natural communication does not always need equal attention for every possible viewpoint.

If the user clearly has a position, the writing can reflect that position while remaining accurate and respectful.

---

## 15. Unnecessary Filler

Common filler patterns include:

- It is important to note that
- It is worth mentioning that
- When it comes to
- At the end of the day
- In today's world
- Needless to say

Remove them if they do not add meaning.

---

## 16. Automatically Correcting Everything

AI may automatically fix:

- Grammar
- Sentence fragments
- Informal wording
- Casual phrasing
- Language mixing

This can remove the user's personality.

Do not "correct" natural communication unless the user asks for correction.

---

## Key Principle

No individual word, punctuation mark, sentence structure, or writing habit proves that something was written by AI.

The goal is not to remove every possible AI-associated pattern.

The goal is to make the writing appropriate for the user, their voice, and the context.
